# ARC community code of conduct

ARC contributors and maintainers commit to a professional, welcoming,
harassment-free community regardless of age, body size, disability, ethnicity,
sex characteristics, gender identity or expression, experience, education,
socioeconomic status, nationality, appearance, race, caste, religion, sexual
identity or orientation, or technical preference.

## Expected behavior

- Be respectful, specific, and evidence-led.
- Critique ideas and changes, not people.
- Respect privacy and never publish credentials, private room data, or personal
  information.
- Accept correction, disclose conflicts, and prioritize community safety.
- Do not use AI-generated volume, impersonation, or fabricated evidence to
  overwhelm review or misrepresent another person.

Harassment, threats, sexualized conduct, discriminatory language, doxxing,
deliberate disruption, and retaliation are unacceptable in project spaces or
when representing the project.

## Reporting and enforcement

Report conduct incidents through the ARC repository's GitHub Private
Vulnerability Reporting channel with a title beginning `[ARC CONDUCT]`.
Although that channel is normally used for security reports, ARC also uses it
as the repository's confidential conduct-report inbox. For platform abuse or
immediate danger, also use the relevant host or local-authority reporting
route. Do not post sensitive incident details in a public issue.

Maintainers will limit access to those needed to assess the report, avoid
conflicted reviewers, preserve relevant evidence, and choose a proportionate
response. Responses may include correction, warning, temporary restriction,
removal, or a permanent ban. This community project promises a fair process,
not a response-time guarantee.

This policy applies to repository, documentation, discussion, event, and other
official project spaces, and to people representing ARC publicly.
